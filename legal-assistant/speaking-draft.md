# PART ONE — URGENT STAY / FIRST DAY BEFORE THE BENCH

---

## OPENING — MENTIONING FOR URGENT ADMISSION

*[When the matter is called or when mentioning for urgency:]*

---

"My Lord, I appear for the Petitioner, The Ministerial Co-operative Society
Limited, Balaghat — a registered co-operative society functioning since
the early 2000s. This is a Writ Petition under Article 226 challenging an
order of the Collector, Balaghat dated 20th July 2026, by which a
decades-old nazul lease has been cancelled overnight and government
possession of the Petitioner's premises has been directed.

My Lord, the matter is extremely urgent. The order directs possession to be
handed over to Government with immediate effect. If that is implemented
before this Court has an opportunity to examine the matter, the Society
will be dispossessed of its only premises, its ongoing construction
activities will be permanently disrupted, and the injury will be wholly
irreversible.

My Lord, I seek urgent ad-interim stay of the impugned order.

May it please Your Lordship to hear the stay application today?"

---

## BRIEF FACTS — FOR THE STAY HEARING

*[Concise, not more than 5 minutes — the Court will read the petition later:]*

---

"My Lord, the facts in brief are these.

**The Society and the land:**
The Petitioner Society holds a nazul lease of a small parcel of land —
1314 square feet — near the Bus Stand, Balaghat, bearing Nazul Sheet No. 306,
Plot No. 277/3. The lease was granted for operating a canteen — described
as 'canteen (vyavsayik)' — i.e., commercial. The Society has been in
peaceful possession of this land for many years.

**What the Society did — lawfully:**
My Lord, over time, the existing structure on the land became old and
dilapidated. The building was lawfully dismantled after a joint inspection
by the Municipal Council and PWD, Balaghat. Thereafter, the Society, in a
duly convened general meeting in February 2025, resolved to construct a
new commercial complex on this land. This is entirely within its
registered bye-laws, which permit the Society to operate canteens and to
let shops on its land.

The Society then obtained —

First — Building plan sanction from the Assistant Director, Town and
Country Planning, Balaghat, dated 20th February 2026. That sanction
contained Condition 1(c), which required the Society to also obtain NOC
from the Nazul Department. The Society was well aware of this condition.

Second — Municipal building permission from the Nagar Palika Parishad,
Balaghat, dated 22nd May 2026.

Third — A tender was floated and a contract awarded to M/s GRM Infratech
through a proper process.

My Lord, at this stage — having obtained Town Planning sanction and
Municipal permission — the Society was preparing to apply for the
Collector's permission, which is required under Lease Condition No. 5
before actual construction. The Society had NOT yet started any
superstructure construction. The only physical work done on site was
the excavation of some column-foundation pits — which the Society
has explained were dug as a protective measure against encroachments
on the vacant plot, pending the Collector's permission.

**What happened — the proceedings:**
My Lord, in May 2026 a complaint was received by the Nazul Officer,
Balaghat. The Society was never shown this complaint. On 23rd May 2026
the Revenue Inspector conducted a site inspection — without any notice
to or presence of the Society. On the very same day, the Nazul Officer
issued a notice giving the Society just THREE DAYS to appear with all
documents.

The Society's President appeared on 27th May 2026 and placed before the
Nazul Officer a detailed written reply along with all available documents
— the Town Planning sanction, the Municipal permission, the tender, the
dismantling certificate.

The Nazul Officer thereafter submitted a detailed adverse report to the
Collector on 3rd July 2026. My Lord, this report was NEVER shared with
the Society. The Society was never called upon to respond to it.

And then, My Lord — on 20th July 2026 — the Collector passed the impugned
order cancelling the entire lease, directing stoppage of all construction,
and directing government takeover of the Demised Premises.

This is the order that is under challenge."

## ARGUMENT ON STAY — THREE PROPOSITIONS

*[For the stay hearing, press three quick, clear points:]*

---

### POINT 1 — PRIMA FACIE CASE

"My Lord, there is a clear prima facie case in our favour on three
independent grounds.

**First: Ex-parte inspection at the foundation of the order.**
The Revenue Inspector's site inspection — which is the foundation of
the entire case against us — was conducted without any notice to the
Society. An adverse report based on an ex-parte inspection and relied
upon to cancel a lease is, prima facie, violative of the principles
of natural justice. An affected party must have an opportunity to be
present and to respond to any inspection that is adverse to its interests.

**Second: Notice of only three days in a lease-cancellation matter.**
My Lord, this is not a case of a minor administrative notice. This is a
proceeding that culminated in cancellation of a decades-old lease.
For such a consequential proceeding, three days' notice is wholly
inadequate. The right of hearing must be meaningful and real, not just
formal. Three days to gather years of records, consult the Society's
committee, arrange documents, and file a response — is no hearing at all.

**Third: The Nazul Officer's final report was never shared.**
The Petitioner replied on 27th May 2026. The Nazul Officer's adverse
report was submitted on 3rd July 2026. The Collector's order is dated
20th July 2026. The Society was never given the Nazul Officer's report
to respond to. That report formed the direct basis of the cancellation
order. A final, decisive report that the party never sees — and is then
condemned by — is a textbook violation of the audi alteram partem rule.

Prima facie, My Lord, three independent natural justice violations
together make out a clear case for this Court's intervention."

---

### POINT 2 — BALANCE OF CONVENIENCE

"My Lord, on balance of convenience:

If the stay is granted — the status quo is maintained. The land remains
with the Society. The Respondents lose nothing. The Government can always
recover possession if this petition fails. No irreversible act occurs.

If the stay is refused — the Society is physically evicted from its
own premises. An institution that has been in existence for over two
decades loses its only premises. The work already done — the Town
Planning sanction, the Municipal permission, the investment in foundation
work — is wasted. The disruption to the Society's members cannot be
undone by a later order in the Society's favour.

My Lord, the balance is entirely in the Petitioner's favour."

---

### POINT 3 — IRREPARABLE INJURY

"My Lord, possession once handed to the Government — in a town like
Balaghat where commercial land is scarce — is practically irreversible.
Unlike money, which can be returned, dispossession of premises causes
a disruption that cannot be fully remedied in damages. If the order is
implemented before this petition is heard, the Society will suffer
irreparable injury.

My Lord, for these three reasons I respectfully submit that an ad-interim
stay of the impugned order may kindly be granted, at least until notice
is served on the Respondents and they file their reply."

## RESPONDING TO ANTICIPATED OBJECTIONS AT STAY STAGE

*[The Government's advocate or the Court may raise these — be prepared:]*

---

**Q: "Why did the Society build without permission?"**

"My Lord, with respect, the Society did NOT construct. The only thing on
the ground was excavation of column pits — foundation pits. No brickwork,
no masonry, no superstructure of any kind. The Society has consistently
maintained this and the Respondents have not disputed it. Digging pits
while awaiting permission is qualitatively different from constructing
a building without permission. The impugned order equates preparatory
site work with completed unauthorised construction — that equation is
wrong in fact and in law."

**Q: "There is an alternative remedy of appeal."**

"My Lord, this Court's jurisdiction under Article 226 is not ousted by
the mere existence of an alternative remedy, particularly where — as
here — the order is alleged to violate fundamental principles of natural
justice and fundamental rights under Article 19(1)(g) and Article 300-A.
The Supreme Court has repeatedly held that violation of natural justice
is a recognised exception to the rule of exhaustion of alternate remedy.
[Advocate to cite relevant SC authority here.]"

**Q: "The Society changed the purpose of the lease."**

"My Lord, the lease was for 'canteen (commercial)' — the key word is
commercial. The proposed structure is also commercial — it includes
canteen and shop facilities, which are expressly within the Society's
bye-laws. The change is in the form of the structure, not in the
nature of the land use. Moreover, the Society's registered bye-laws
explicitly permit operating shops on Society land on rent. Any change
of use was merely incidental and was in any case for the Society to
seek prior permission for — which it was intending to do. The response
to an application for permission should not be pre-empted by cancellation."

# PART TWO — MAIN HEARING (MERITS)

---

## OPENING FOR MERITS

"My Lord, this petition raises a straightforward but serious question:
whether a decades-old nazul lease can be cancelled — without sharing
the final adverse report with the affected party, on the basis of an
ex-parte site inspection, on three days' notice — merely because the
lessee excavated column pits on vacant land while waiting to apply
for construction permission.

We submit the answer must be no. The impugned order cannot survive
scrutiny on facts, on procedure, or on proportionality."

---

## GROUND I — NATURAL JUSTICE (MOST IMPORTANT)

*[Argue this ground first and most fully:]*

---

"My Lord, I begin with natural justice because it is decisive. If the
procedure by which the order was made was fundamentally flawed, this
Court need not go further.

**Sub-ground (a): Ex-parte inspection**

My Lord, on 23rd May 2026 the Revenue Inspector visited the site without
any prior notice to the Society. He found column pits on the land and
recorded this in his report. This report was then used by the Nazul
Officer as the factual foundation for his recommendation, and by the
Collector as the factual foundation for the impugned order.

My Lord, the Society was present on its own land every day. There was
no reason — administrative or otherwise — not to give it notice of the
inspection. A simple notice would have allowed the Society's
representative to accompany the Inspector, to explain the nature of
the foundation pits, and to clarify that no actual construction had
commenced. That opportunity was denied.

The law is settled: where an inspection is to form the basis of an
adverse order having civil consequences, the affected party must be
given an opportunity to participate or at least to respond to the
inspection report before any order is passed.

**Sub-ground (b): Three days' notice**

My Lord, the notice dated 23rd May 2026 — issued on the same day as
the ex-parte inspection — gave the Society three days to appear with
all construction documents and explanations.

Three days — for a co-operative body that has to convene a meeting
of its committee, locate its records, obtain copies of documents,
consult its advocate, and prepare a response — is not a real
opportunity. It is a formal notice masquerading as a hearing.

The principle is well established: the opportunity to be heard must be
adequate in time and content. In a matter with such drastic civil
consequences — cancellation of a long-standing lease — the Society
was entitled to a meaningful, reasonable opportunity. Three days is
not that.

**Sub-ground (c): Complaint not disclosed**

My Lord, the proceedings were initiated on a complaint dated 13th May
2026. The Society was never told who complained, what was complained
about, or what the specific allegations were. The Society was therefore
unable to rebut the precise case being made against it. Reliance upon
undisclosed complaints to support an adverse order is contrary to the
principles of fair procedure.

**Sub-ground (d): Final adverse report not shared — this is the critical one**

My Lord, this is perhaps the most glaring violation. After the Society
submitted its detailed reply on 27th May 2026, the proceedings ought to
have concluded — either in the Society's favour or against it. Instead,
on 3rd July 2026 — over a month later — the Nazul Officer submitted a
fresh, detailed adverse report to the Collector. This report ran to
several pages, addressed every point raised by the Society, rejected the
Society's explanation, and recommended cancellation.

This report was NEVER shown to the Society. It was never put to the
Society for its response. It was not a communication of the final
decision — it was fresh adverse material placed before the deciding
authority. The Collector then based his cancellation order directly
on this report.

My Lord, this is textbook denial of natural justice. The rule in audi
alteram partem is not satisfied merely by giving a notice at the
beginning of proceedings. It requires that the party be heard on
every material piece of adverse information that is placed before the
authority before a decision is taken. Here, the most important
document — the Nazul Officer's final adverse recommendation — was
withheld from the Society. The Society was condemned on the basis of
material it never saw.

An order passed on undisclosed adverse material is void.
[Advocate to cite relevant Supreme Court and MP HC authorities here.]

**Conclusion on natural justice:**

My Lord, taken together — ex-parte inspection, three-day notice,
undisclosed complaint, undisclosed final report — these four violations
permeate the entire proceeding from beginning to end. The Collector's
order rests on a foundation that is procedurally infirm from the
first step to the last. It cannot stand."

---

## GROUND II — DISPROPORTIONALITY

"My Lord, even if one were to ignore the procedural violations —
which I respectfully submit would be impossible — the Collector's order
is grossly disproportionate to the alleged infraction.

My Lord, consider what the Society actually did:

— It obtained Town Planning sanction on 20th February 2026.
— It obtained Municipal building permission on 22nd May 2026.
— It awarded a contract through a proper tender.
— It dug column pits on its own leasehold land.

And consider what it had NOT done:

— It had not erected a single wall, a single column, a single slab.
— It had not occupied any additional land.
— It had not sublet the premises to third parties.
— It had not violated any order of a court.

The 'violation', at its highest, was: preliminary site preparation
without first obtaining the Collector's permission under Lease Condition
No. 5.

My Lord, the proportionate response to such a violation — where no
superstructure exists, where all municipal and planning permissions are
in place, where the lessee has been on the land for decades — is
NOT cancellation of the lease. The proportionate response would be a
direction to stop work, apply for permission, and regularise. Or
at most a notice to show cause before any drastic action.

Cancelling the entire lease, directing stoppage of all activities, and
ordering government takeover — as the very first and final response —
is a sledgehammer applied to a crack. It is not the law.

The doctrine of proportionality — which this Court and the Supreme Court
have consistently applied to administrative action — requires that the
severity of the response must be commensurate with the gravity of the
breach. The impugned order fails this test comprehensively.
[Advocate to cite relevant Supreme Court authority on proportionality
in administrative action and specifically in lease/tenancy cases.]"

## GROUND III — FACTUAL AND LEGAL ERRORS

"My Lord, I now address specific factual and legal errors in the
impugned order.

**Error 1: Canteen versus commercial complex is not a change of land use**

My Lord, the Collector has held that converting a 'canteen (commercial)'
to a 'commercial shopping complex' amounts to a fundamental change of
purpose requiring permission under the MP Government Memo of 1974.

With respect, this is incorrect. Both uses are commercial in nature.
The lease itself characterises the use as 'vyavsayik' — commercial.
The proposed complex is also commercial. The Society's own registered
bye-laws permit running canteens AND operating shops on its land.
A complex that houses a canteen and shops is squarely within both
the lease purpose and the Society's authorised objects.

A change of use — in the context of the 1974 Government Memo — 
contemplates a material change from one category to another, for
example from commercial to residential, or from one to a fundamentally
incompatible use. Two commercial uses, both within the Society's
registered objectives, are not a change of land use.

**Error 2: The demolition of the old building cannot be turned against
the Society**

The Collector records the demolition of the old building as a factor.
But the demolition was lawful — conducted with joint inspection by the
Municipal Council and PWD. A lessee cannot be penalised for lawfully
removing an unsafe structure; that removal does not extinguish the
right to reconstruct, subject to appropriate permissions.

**Error 3: Foundation pits are not 'construction'**

Lease Condition No. 5 requires permission for 'construction'. Digging
foundation column pits — without any brickwork or superstructure — is
preparatory, preliminary site work. It is not 'construction' in any
technical, legal or common sense. The Collector has equated preliminary
site preparation with completed unauthorised construction. This is a
perverse finding on the facts.

**Error 4: The Society was NOT running the canteen only because the
old building was demolished**

The Collector holds against the Society that it is not running a canteen
as per its objects and the lease purpose. But My Lord, the canteen is
not running precisely because the old building was demolished and the
new one is being planned. The cessation of activity during a construction
interval is temporary and bona fide — it is not abandonment of the
lease purpose."

---

## GROUND IV — FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS (BRIEFLY)

"My Lord, I briefly mention the constitutional dimension.

The Petitioner Society and its members have the right to carry on
trade and commercial activity under Article 19(1)(g) of the
Constitution. Cancelling the lease on disproportionate grounds
and on a procedure vitiated by natural justice violations is a
restriction on that right that is neither reasonable nor in
accordance with law.

Further, My Lord, Article 300-A provides that no person shall be
deprived of property save by authority of law. An order that violates
natural justice is not 'authority of law' in the sense of Article 300-A.
The dispossession directed by the impugned order therefore violates
Article 300-A.

My Lord, for these reasons — procedural invalidity, disproportionality,
factual errors, and constitutional infirmity — the impugned order
is liable to be quashed in its entirety."

---

## CLOSING SUBMISSION

"My Lord, to summarise the Petitioner's case in a few sentences:

A co-operative society of government employees, holding a nazul lease
for over two decades, obtained Town Planning sanction and Municipal
building permission to renovate their premises. While preparing the
site and awaiting the Collector's permission — which they always
intended to seek — an anonymous complaint was filed. The Revenue
Inspector inspected the site ex-parte, and on the basis of that
ex-parte report, without ever sharing the Nazul Officer's final adverse
recommendation with the Society, the Collector cancelled the entire
lease and directed government takeover.

My Lord, the procedure was wrong. The finding was disproportionate.
The factual basis was incorrect. And the constitutional rights of the
Society and its members have been violated.

We respectfully pray that this Hon'ble Court be pleased to:

One — Quash and set aside the impugned order dated 20th July 2026;

Two — Restrain the Respondents from dispossessing the Society from
the Demised Premises;

Three — Direct the Respondents to consider the Society's application
for permission under Lease Condition No. 5 on merits; and

Four — Grant costs.

I am grateful to Your Lordship."
