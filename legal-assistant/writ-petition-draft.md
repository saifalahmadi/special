   natural justice; (b) perversity and non-application of mind;
   (c) gross disproportionality; and (d) patent illegality. It is

FACTS OF THE CASE

1.    The Petitioner is The Ministerial Co-operative Society Limited, a
      co-operative body constituted and registered under the Madhya
      Pradesh Co-operative Societies Act, 1960 (hereinafter "the 1960
      Act"). The Petitioner has been in existence for several decades
      and its objectives, as set out in its registered Bye-laws
      (Nos. 3(1) to 3(6)), include: running canteens for general public
      in district and tehsil offices, operating shops on a rental basis
      on its land, purchasing and selling food articles and essential
      commodities for its members, and otherwise promoting the welfare
      and self-reliance of its members.

2.    The State of Madhya Pradesh / the Nazul Department granted the
      Petitioner a lease of nazul land bearing Nazul Sheet No. 306,
      Plot No. 277/3, area 122.075 sq. mtrs. (approximately 1314 sq. ft.)
      near Bus Stand, Balaghat, for the specific purpose of operating a
      canteen (commercial) (hereinafter "the Demised Premises"). A true
      copy of the lease deed along with its terms and conditions is
      annexed as ANNEXURE P-1.

3.    Over the years, the Petitioner lawfully used the Demised Premises
      in accordance with the lease. The existing structure on the
      Demised Premises became old, dilapidated and structurally unsafe.
      After a joint inspection by the Nagar Palika Parishad, Balaghat
      and the PWD, Balaghat, the dilapidated structure was lawfully
      dismantled. Copies of the Dismantling Order and the inspection
      report are annexed as ANNEXURE P-2.

4.    In its general meeting held in February 2025, the Petitioner's
      members unanimously resolved to construct a new commercial complex
      on the Demised Premises, to replace the demolished building and to
      further the society's commercial purposes including canteen and
      shop facilities for its members. A copy of the resolution is
      annexed as ANNEXURE P-3.

5.    In furtherance of this resolution, the Petitioner applied for and
      obtained building plan sanction from the Asst. Director, Town &
      Country Planning (Nagar tatha Gram Niwesh), Balaghat on
      20.02.2026. The said NOC expressly stipulated at Condition 1(c)
      that the Petitioner must also obtain NOC/permission from the
      Nazul Department before commencing construction. The Petitioner
      was aware of this condition and accordingly intended to seek the
      Collector's permission, as required under Lease Condition No. 5,
      before undertaking any actual construction. A copy of the
      Town Planning NOC dated 20.02.2026 is annexed as ANNEXURE P-4.

6.    The Petitioner also applied for and obtained building construction
      permission from the Nagar Palika Parishad, Balaghat dated
      22.05.2026. A copy of the Municipal permission is annexed as
      ANNEXURE P-5.

7.    A formal tender process was conducted and a construction contract
      was entered into with M/s GRM Infratech, the lowest bidder. A
      copy of the tender and contract is annexed as ANNEXURE P-6.

8.    At the time of the impugned proceedings, the Petitioner had NOT
      commenced any superstructure construction. The only preparatory
      work on the Demised Premises was the excavation of column-pit
      foundations (i.e., pits dug for laying column foundations),
      which the Petitioner undertook: (a) as a preliminary protective
      measure against encroachments on the vacant land; and
      (b) in anticipation of obtaining the Collector's permission.
      No brickwork, masonry, concrete or any superstructure had been
      erected.

9.    On 13.05.2026, a complaint was received by the Nazul Officer,
      Balaghat. Neither the complainant's identity nor the specific
      allegations in the complaint were ever made known to the
      Petitioner. On 23.05.2026 — the same day that the Nazul Officer
      directed a site inspection — a notice (Letter No. 49/Nazul/2026)
      was issued to the Petitioner giving it only THREE DAYS to appear
      with all construction documents. The site inspection by the
      Revenue Inspector was conducted ex-parte, without any notice to
      or presence of the Petitioner.

10.   The Petitioner's President appeared before the Nazul Officer on
      27.05.2026 and submitted a detailed written explanation together
      with copies of: the building plan sanction, the Municipal NOC,
      the tender and contract, the dismantling certificate, and other
      relevant documents. The Petitioner categorically stated that no
      actual construction had commenced and that it was awaiting the
      Collector's permission.

11.   Without conducting any further enquiry into the Petitioner's
      explanation and documents, and without giving the Petitioner any
      further opportunity, the Nazul Officer submitted his detailed
      report dated 03.07.2026 to the Collector recommending cancellation
      of the lease. The Petitioner was not furnished a copy of this
      report nor called upon to respond to it.

12.   The Collector, Balaghat, passed the impugned order dated
      20.07.2026 in Case No.___/B-121/2026-27, whereby:
      (a) the Petitioner's lease over the Demised Premises was
      cancelled in its entirety;
      (b) all construction activity on the site was directed to be
      stopped with immediate effect;
      (c) Government possession of the Demised Premises was ordered
      to be secured.

13.   The Collector's order relies on three broad grounds:
      (i) Violation of Lease Condition No. 5 (construction without
      prior permission of the Collector);
      (ii) Change of sanctioned purpose (from canteen to commercial
      shopping complex) without permission under the MP Government
      Memo dated 03.01.1974; and
      (iii) Failure to obtain NOC from the Nazul Department before
      commencement of construction.

14.   The Petitioner submits that the impugned order is erroneous on
      facts and law, violates fundamental principles of natural justice
      and proportionality, and is liable to be quashed and set aside
      in its entirety. The Petitioner therefore approaches this
      Hon'ble Court under Articles 226 and 227 of the Constitution
      of India.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

GROUNDS FOR CHALLENGE

I. VIOLATION OF PRINCIPLES OF NATURAL JUSTICE

A. Site inspection conducted ex-parte without notice

15.   The Revenue Inspector's site inspection on 23.05.2026 — which
      forms the foundation of the entire proceedings — was conducted
      ex-parte, without any prior notice to or presence of the
      Petitioner. An adverse inspection report formed the basis of the
      Nazul Officer's recommendation and consequently the Collector's
      order. The Petitioner was never given an opportunity to be present
      during the inspection, to draw attention to the true nature of
      the foundation pits, or to demonstrate that no superstructure
      construction had begun.

      It is a settled principle of administrative law that any
      inspection or inquiry that forms the basis of an adverse order
      having civil consequences must afford the affected party a
      reasonable opportunity of representation. An ex-parte inspection
      report based on which civil rights are extinguished is contrary
      to the principles of natural justice. [Advocate to verify and
      cite applicable MP HC / Supreme Court precedent on ex-parte
      inspection as violative of natural justice.]

B. Grossly inadequate notice period — three days

16.   The notice dated 23.05.2026 required the Petitioner to appear
      within THREE DAYS with all construction permissions and documents.
      Three days is a wholly insufficient and unreasonable timeframe
      within which a co-operative society — having to consult its
      office-bearers, locate historical records, and arrange legal
      counsel — can meaningfully exercise its right to be heard. Such
      curtailed notice renders the ostensible hearing an empty
      formality rather than a genuine opportunity.

      The rule of audi alteram partem (no person should be condemned
      unheard) requires not only that notice be given, but that the
      notice be adequate in content, clarity, and time, so as to enable
      the party to present its case meaningfully. A three-day notice
      in a matter that resulted in cancellation of a lease of many
      years' standing falls woefully short of this standard.

C. Complaint not disclosed to Petitioner

17.   The complaint dated 13.05.2026, which triggered the entire
      proceeding, was never disclosed to the Petitioner. The Petitioner
      does not know the identity of the complainant, the precise
      allegations made, or the materials placed before the Nazul
      Officer at the inception of the proceedings. The Petitioner
      was therefore unable to rebut allegations it was never formally
      informed of. Reliance upon an undisclosed complaint to initiate
      adverse action violates the Petitioner's right to a fair hearing.

D. Nazul Officer's report not furnished; no opportunity to respond

18.   After the Petitioner submitted its detailed reply on 27.05.2026,
      the Nazul Officer submitted a detailed adverse report to the
      Collector on 03.07.2026. This report formed the direct basis
      of the Collector's decision to cancel the lease. The Petitioner
      was neither furnished a copy of this report nor called upon to
      submit its response to it before the Collector passed the
      impugned order. The failure to share this critical report
      with the Petitioner before passing the final order is a material
      breach of natural justice, rendering the order liable to be
      set aside.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

II. THE ORDER IS DISPROPORTIONATE AND HARSH

19.   The Petitioner emphasises that at the time of the impugned order:
      (a) No superstructure whatsoever had been erected on the
      Demised Premises;
      (b) The only physical work done was excavation of column pits —
      a preliminary protective and preparatory step;
      (c) The Petitioner had already obtained Town Planning sanction
      and Municipal building permission;
      (d) The Petitioner was specifically waiting for the Collector's
      permission, as stipulated in Lease Condition No. 5 and in the
      Town Planning NOC, before commencing actual construction.

20.   The doctrine of proportionality demands that administrative
      action must be proportionate to the gravity of the wrong sought
      to be remedied. Even assuming that excavating foundation pits
      amounted to a technical violation of Lease Condition No. 5 —
      which the Petitioner denies — the response of permanently
      cancelling a long-standing lease, stopping all activity and
      directing government takeover of the land is wholly
      disproportionate to the alleged violation.

21.   The Collector could have achieved the regulatory objective of
      ensuring prior permission through lesser measures such as:
      (a) directing the Petitioner to stop work and apply for
      permission within a fixed time;
      (b) granting the Petitioner an opportunity to regularise the
      construction with appropriate conditions;
      (c) imposing a penalty or compounding the violation;
      rather than resorting to the drastic and irreversible remedy
      of lease cancellation.

22.   Lease cancellation, being an extreme civil consequence that
      deprives the Petitioner of its long-standing right to the
      Demised Premises and effectively puts the Society out of its
      premises, must be resorted to only in cases of grave, wilful
      and sustained breach. The present case discloses no such grave
      breach — the Petitioner had legitimate approvals, had sought
      proper permissions, and had merely excavated preparatory pits
      while waiting for the Collector's nod. The impugned order is
      grossly disproportionate to the facts on record.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

III. FACTUAL AND LEGAL ERRORS IN THE ORDER

A. Canteen and commercial complex are not categorically different uses

23.   The impugned order holds that conversion from "canteen
      (commercial)" to a "commercial shopping complex" amounts to a
      fundamental change of purpose requiring permission under the MP
      Government Memo dated 03.01.1974. This finding is erroneous.
      The lease expressly characterises the permitted use as "canteen
      (vyavsayik)" i.e., canteen — commercial. A new commercial complex
      — which would in any event include a canteen and shops — is not a
      categorically different "land use" but a development of the same
      commercial use. The change, if any, is in the form of the
      structure and the scale of the commercial activity, not in the
      nature of land use. Moreover, the Petitioner's registered bye-laws
      explicitly permit operating shops on its land on a rental basis
      (Bye-law 3(3)). The proposed complex is squarely within the scope
      of the Society's authorised purposes.

B. Old building demolished with lawful authority

24.   The impugned order records the previous dismantling of the
      building and treats the resultant vacant plot as supporting
      the Petitioner's supposed plan to change the land use. The
      demolition was, however, lawfully carried out after joint
      inspection by the Municipal Council and the PWD. Lawful
      demolition of a dilapidated structure creates no adverse
      inference against the lessee's right to reconstruct. A
      Petitioner who lawfully demolishes a dilapidated building
      cannot be penalised for the demolition and then also penalised
      for attempting to reconstruct.

C. Excavation of pits does not constitute "construction" within the
   meaning of Lease Condition No. 5

25.   Lease Condition No. 5 requires the Collector's prior permission
      for "construction" (nirman). The Petitioner respectfully submits
      that excavation of column pits for the purpose of protective
      demarcation and as a preliminary site-preparation step, without
      any placement of concrete, masonry or structural elements, does
      not constitute "construction" of a building in the ordinary,
      legal, or technical sense. The Petitioner has never denied
      excavating these pits; it has consistently maintained that no
      actual construction was commenced. The impugned order conflates
      a minor preparatory act with full-blown unauthorised construction,
      which is a perverse reading of the facts.

D. Municipal NOC was not obtained by concealment of material facts

26.   The impugned order records that the Petitioner concealed from the
      Nagar Palika the requirement of Nazul Department NOC when applying
      for Municipal building permission. The Petitioner denies that it
      acted in bad faith. At the time of the Municipal application, the
      Town Planning NOC (Condition 1(c)) had already been issued — and
      that NOC itself made the Nazul Department permission a pre-
      condition. The Petitioner intended to apply for Nazul/Collector
      permission after securing the Municipal NOC and the Town Planning
      sanction, as a sequential process. Characterising this procedural
      sequence as concealment of material facts is incorrect and
      unwarranted.

E. Society functioning within its bye-laws

27.   The impugned order records that the Petitioner is not carrying out
      any commercial activity in accordance with its bye-laws (since the
      canteen is not currently operational). The Petitioner submits that
      the reason the canteen is not operational is precisely that the
      old building was demolished in the process of planned
      reconstruction. The Society intends to resume and expand its
      canteen and commercial operations once the new structure is built.
      An interim cessation of activity pending lawful reconstruction
      cannot be treated as permanent abandonment of the Society's
      purposes or a violation of its bye-laws.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

IV. VIOLATION OF FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS

28.   The Petitioner's members, being Government employees, have a
      legitimate interest in the Petitioner Society's canteen and shop
      services. The Society itself, as a co-operative body, has the
      right to carry on lawful commercial activity protected under
      Article 19(1)(g) of the Constitution of India. The impugned
      order extinguishes the Petitioner's right to the Demised Premises
      and consequently its ability to function, on grounds that are
      disproportionate and not supported by law. The impugned order
      therefore violates Article 19(1)(g) of the Constitution.

29.   Moreover, the Petitioner has a right to property in the leasehold
      interest in the Demised Premises. Deprivation of such interest by
      an order that is procedurally and substantively defective is
      contrary to Article 300-A of the Constitution, which provides
      that no person shall be deprived of property save by authority
      of law. An order vitiated by non-compliance with natural justice
      is not "authority of law" within the meaning of Article 300-A.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

V. QUESTION OF ALTERNATE REMEDY

30.   While the Petitioner is aware that under the Madhya Pradesh Land
      Revenue Code, 1959, certain statutory appellate remedies may be
      available against the Collector's orders, this Hon'ble Court's
      jurisdiction under Article 226 is not ousted by the mere
      availability of an alternate remedy. It is a well-settled
      principle of constitutional law that the existence of an
      alternative remedy does not operate as an absolute bar to the
      maintainability of a writ petition, particularly where:

      (a) the order impugned is violative of fundamental principles
      of natural justice;
      (b) the order is ex-facie without jurisdiction or in excess of
      jurisdiction;
      (c) fundamental rights are alleged to have been infringed;
      (d) the impugned order causes irreparable injury and the
      alternate remedy is not equally efficacious.

      The present petition satisfies all the above conditions.
      Moreover, the impugned order requires immediate intervention
      since the direction to take Government possession of the
      Demised Premises would, if implemented, cause irreversible
      and irreparable harm to the Petitioner and its members.
